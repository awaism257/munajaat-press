Heroes of Islam Manga Series

The great figures of Islamic history, drawn as manga and told from the medieval chronicles. In keeping with Islamic tradition, no Prophet or Companion is depicted.

The Shield of Al-Aqsa, Volume 1

Born in Exile

Tikrit, 1137. A family flees in the night, and a son is born on the road. Yūsuf ibn Ayyūb grows up in the shadow of greater men: his patient father Ayyūb, his fierce uncle Shīrkūh, and Nūr ad-Dīn Zangī, the just ruler who meant to unite the Muslim world and free Jerusalem. From the walls of Damascus he watches the Second Crusade break in four days, until a desperate exile arrives from Egypt and he is ordered onto a road he never wished to take. History would remember him as Ṣalāḥ ad-Dīn, Salahuddin.

  • Drawn from the chronicles of Ibn Shaddād, Ibn al-Athīr and Abū Shāma; dialogue and some scenes are dramatised, and the book says so
  • A map, timeline, Who’s Who, historical notes, glossary and list of sources at the back

The Shield of Al-Aqsa, Volume 2

The Road to Egypt

Egypt, 1164. Shāwar, the deposed vizier of Egypt, swore that if Nūr ad-Dīn gave him an army he would share the riches of the Nile. Asad ad-Dīn Shīrkūh led that army across the desert, and his nephew Yūsuf rode beside him, against his own wishes. They won Shāwar his office back within weeks; before the summer was out he had called the Franks of Jerusalem against them. Three years later they returned, to the battle of al-Bābayn and the siege of Alexandria.

  • Drawn from the chronicles of Ibn Shaddād, Ibn al-Athīr and al-Maqrīzī; dialogue and some scenes are dramatised, and the book says so
